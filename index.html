Projects

X HORIZON — Explore Nature Beyond the Horizon

Visit the X HORIZON website

X HORIZON is an astronomy and space-science learning ecosystem founded to make high-quality learning accessible to enthusiasts, including learners who cannot afford expensive educational platforms. Its purpose is to connect structured study, practical skills, projects, simulations, scientific resources, and community in one coherent environment. Learning and certificates are intended to remain free, without paid course subscriptions or paywalls; voluntary donations may support the project.

Platform Architecture

The system has two primary components: the main website, which acts as the gateway to the ecosystem, and a dedicated study-material environment accessible through the site or the /slides route. The study environment is intended to organize substantial educational material into course-like and book-like resources rather than disconnected pages.

The main learning entry point is Concepts. It contains 17 astronomy and space-science terminology categories, all initially available, with Beginner, Normal, and Advanced levels. Learners progress through articles by passing timed written assessments. Each quiz contains five AI-generated written-answer questions and uses fullscreen and focus-loss safeguards. Passing unlocks the next article; completing a category earns its unique certificate, and completing all categories qualifies the learner for a broader achievement certificate.

Completion and certificate records are stored through the Firebase project X HORIZON CERTIFICATIONS. The intended system recognizes saved category completion across devices, so completed categories remain unlocked and certificate eligibility reflects recorded progress.

Learning Ecosystem

Learn: Concepts, structured study materials, Skills, Python Learning, and guided Learning Paths.

Practice: Exercises and technical work that connect scientific ideas with calculations, programming, and data analysis.

Build: Projects, physics and space simulations, and practical explorations of space technology and missions.

Progress: Persistent learning records, assessment-based certificates, and continued development through the community.

The ecosystem also connects learners with real scientific resources and interactive experiences, including International Space Station material, the NASA Exoplanet Archive, and JPL Eyes.
